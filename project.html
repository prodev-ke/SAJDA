What We Are Building

The Project

One Centre of Excellence. Eight strengthened feeder centres. And a Waqf endowment built to carry all of it, long after the last wall is rendered.

Three Commitments

From groundwork to endowment.

The project is deliberately built in three parts. Each one is useless without the other two: a building with no feeder schools stands empty, and a school with no endowment closes the moment the appeal ends.

01

Construct a permanent Islamic Integrated Education Complex

A purpose-built campus replacing the temporary mabati structures and borrowed mosque spaces in use today — the Sagante Jaldessa Islamic Education Centre, carrying the national curriculum alongside authentic Islamic values.

Main Centre of Excellence
02

Strengthen the infrastructure of the existing eight madrasa centres

Upgrading the facilities already serving children across Sagante Jaldesa Ward today. These eight centres are the feeder network — they teach the ibtidai classes and send their pupils on to the main centre.

8 Feeder Centres · Grade 1–6
03

Establish a Waqf Endowment Fund of KSH 80,000,000

A five-storey rental building whose income secures long-term, sustainable support for centre administration, teachers' salaries, maintenance and the ongoing sustainability of the institution — beyond the initial build.

The Main Centre

Sagante Jaldessa Islamic Education Centre

Today a child in this ward finishes Grade 6 and has nowhere to continue. The Centre of Excellence closes that gap, carrying learners from intermediate study all the way to full Qur'an memorisation.

Mutawasit Classes

Grade 7 – 9. Intermediate Islamic and general education, taking pupils straight on from the ibtidai classes at the eight feeder centres.

Tanawi Classes

Grade 10 – 12. Advanced Islamic and general education, carrying students through to the completion of their secondary studies.

Tahfeed Centre

Qur'an memorisation. Nurturing huffaaz for the Ummah, through both full-time and part-time programmes.

Why a single centre of excellence

Eight small centres cannot each carry a full secondary syllabus, a tahfeed programme and qualified specialist teachers. Concentrating that provision in one properly built campus is the only way this ward gets teaching of real quality.

The eight centres remain exactly where they are, close to the homes of the youngest children. They feed the main centre. Nothing is closed, and no child has to travel far in the early years.

Alongside the classrooms sits a Deen Teachers Training Campus, so that the teachers staffing this institution are raised from within the community it serves.

The Waqf

An endowment, not an appeal that has to be repeated.

A five-storey rental building, held as waqf, generating sustainable income for the centre for as long as it stands.

Most education appeals raise enough to put up a building and nothing to run it. Salaries go unpaid, maintenance is deferred, and within a few years the building is in worse condition than the mabati it replaced.

The Waqf exists precisely to prevent that. Rental income from the property funds four things permanently:

  • ◆ Centre administration
  • ◆ Teachers' salaries
  • ◆ Maintenance of the buildings
  • ◆ Long-term sustainability of the institution
Waqf Endowment Target
KSH 80,000,000
